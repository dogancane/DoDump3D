Validation benchmark (3D silo clogging)

Runs this engine on identical dry spheres discharging from a flat-bottomed silo through a round orifice, 6 seeds each at orifice / sphere diameter 3, 4 and 6. Published 3D experiments (Zuriguel et al. 2005) find jams after a few to a few tens of grains at D/d = 3 and no jams above about 4.9 for spheres. Passing shows the 3D contact, friction and arching mechanics reproduce the clogging trend; it does not test water forces or rock shape. The silo is small (about 250 to 420 grains), so it checks the trend, not the exact critical size.

Validation and diagnostics

Is this physics or numerics?

Treat a result as possibly meaningful only if: the count and mass balances close, maximum overlap stays below about 5 % of radius, no stones escape through walls, and the same outcome appears with a different seed, a halved time step and stiffer contacts. Use the buttons to rerun with one change at a time and compare in Results.

3D method, assumptions and status

What changes from the 2D page

  • Full pipe, no slice. Stones move in the round pipe with 6 degrees of freedom. The whole mass flow is released, so masses and rates are real tonnes and t/h; no slice factor.
  • Stones. Same grade sampler and shape classes as 2D (mass, Dn, Zingg classes, volume factor, Lmax cap, measured sub-population). The body is a superellipsoid |x/a|^p + |y/b|^p + |z/c|^p = 1 inside the L x B x T box, with p set so the volume is the volume factor times L B T: p = 2.33 for blocks at 0.6 (flat-ish faces, rounded corners), p = 2 for rounded and L/T stones (ellipsoid). Every contact sphere is inscribed in that body, chosen to cover its surface (up to 24 spheres, symmetric, axis tips weighted so L, B and T are reached: on average 97 to 99 % of each half axis). The drawn stone is the same body. Inertia from the superellipsoid. Orientation is a uniform random quaternion.
  • Correction in 0.2. Version 0.1 put 8 spheres at the corners of the full bounding box. A stone 720 mm long was then 830 mm across its diagonal, wider than a real rock with that volume factor, and wider than the drawn stone. Stones smaller than the opening wedged across the bucket lip. At 4000 t/h with 800 mm bucket bottoms, 6 of 6 seeds blocked with v0.1, 5 of them on single stones smaller than the passage; with v0.2, 5 of 6 flow and the sixth is a multi-stone dome. The dome card now lists the jammed stone's L x B x T against the passage.
  • Walls. Each bucket, wall thickness, lip, insertion ledge, the feed chute or funnel and the restart gate is the same closed quadrilateral as in 2D, revolved about the axis. A sphere meets a revolved segment at the closest point in its own meridian plane, which is exact for surfaces of revolution.
  • Contacts. Same laws as 2D: linear spring-dashpot normal force from the restitution coefficient, incremental tangential spring with the Coulomb limit (a 3D vector kept in the tangent plane by projection and rescaling every step), constant rolling torque opposing the relative spin, stiffness scaled by stone size and by E* of the wall material.
  • Rotation. Unit quaternions, Euler's equations in the body frame with the gyroscopic term, semi-implicit Euler.
  • Time step. A contact on a clump also turns the stone, so the effective mass at the contact is m / (1 + m d^2 / I). The shortest contact time uses the smallest value of that over the stone sample, which roughly halves the time step against the 2D rule. Without it, settled packs did not come to rest and high damping became unstable.
  • Water. The same 1D coupled water column along the whole string (entrained flow, joint leakage, free surface in the moonpool, Di Felice hindrance, pressure gradient, added mass). The local voidage is the stone volume in the cross-section (2D used 2/3 of the in-plane area fraction).
  • Pipe tilt. As in 2D the DEM pipe stays straight and gravity turns with the local link angle; stones settle towards -x.
  • Feed (0.3). "Drop" releases stones at rest across the feed opening, falling straight; they barely touch the walls in the moonpool. "Conveyor discharge" releases a stream at the feed offset with the belt speed horizontally (direction in the pipe section, scatter of speed and direction), the vertical speed of the drop below the head pulley and forward spin (share of v/r). Stones then hit the funnel or the first bucket wall on the far side and arrive at the water tumbling, as in operation.
  • Vessel motion (0.3). Regular roll, pitch and heave with random phases per run. In the pipe frame the apparent gravity is turned by the roll and pitch angles, the pipe at depth z below the roll centre accelerates sideways by z times the angular acceleration (the inertial term +z w^2 theta opposes the gravity tilt below z = g / w^2), and heave changes apparent gravity by (g + h'')/g, also for buoyancy. The string moves rigidly with the vessel; its own dynamic response, sloshing of the water column and current-induced motion are not modelled.
  • Numerical precision (0.3). Presets for the contact stiffness and time step. High (default): overlap at most 1 % of Dn50 at the terminal velocity of Dn90, dt at most 0.10 t_c. Very high: overlap 0.5 % at the water entry speed of the dry fall, dt 0.07 t_c. The values used are listed under Derived values; compare Standard and High on a case to see whether a result depends on the numerics.
  • Outputs (0.3). Wall impacts per bucket (new contacts with normal speed above 0.3 m/s: count, energy, peak speed) for wear and lip loading, and the speed and long-axis angle of stones crossing the water surface (60 deg median = random orientation).
  • Collisions in water. Wet restitution depends on the Stokes number St = rho_s v d / (9 mu) (Joseph et al. 2001); it approaches the dry value above St of about 1000. Rock impacts here have St of about 10^4 to 10^5, so the dry restitution input applies and no lubrication correction is added.
  • Rock-wall friction. Interface shear tests of granular soil on smooth HDPE geomembranes report friction angles of roughly 15 to 25 deg (mu about 0.27 to 0.47). The HDPE default 0.3 sits in that range. It is still an assumption for wet quarried rock on a bucket wall: use the Probability tab's friction sensitivity to see how much it matters for a given case.
  • Arches. In a round pipe a dome must lean on the wall all round. The engine records the azimuth of each wall contact in 12 sectors of 30 deg; a connected group of stalled stones spans the pipe when its wall contacts touch at least 3 sectors and are not confined to one half of the circumference. A single stone meeting that test is a single-stone obstruction. Status levels, events and run classes are otherwise identical to 2D.
  • Solid fraction. Volume fraction in each axial bin. The dense-zone threshold default is 0.35 (2D: 0.5 in-plane).

Checks run on the engine

  • 3D silo clogging benchmark (Probability tab): identical dry spheres through a round orifice. At D/d = 3 all runs jammed after 4 to 70 grains; at D/d = 4 and 6 the silo emptied to its dead zone without a loaded arch, matching the trend of Zuriguel et al. 2005 (no jams above about 4.9 for spheres).
  • Single sphere in still water reaches the Newton-drag terminal velocity exactly (3.065 m/s for the test case, analytic 3.065 m/s), with no lateral drift.
  • 40 stones settling on the closed restart gate come to rest: kinetic energy from about 250 J to below 0.001 J, highest speed 3 mm/s, overlap about 1 % of stone radius, also with restitution 0.1.
  • Count and mass balance close to zero in every run; no stones escaped in the test runs.

Not yet done

  • No validation against 3D clogging experiments (for example Janda et al. 2015 narrow pipes, Thomas and Durian 2015 hoppers). Read single runs as mechanisms; compare seeds before drawing conclusions.
  • The body is a smooth superellipsoid; real rock faces and sharp edges are approximated by its exponent. The sphere fill lies inside the body, so contact is never larger than the stone (gap to the surface on average about 12 % of T/2).
  • No radial water velocity profile, wakes or CFD coupling (level 3).
  • The string editor and grade import stay on the 2D page for now. The 3D engine uses the same module interfaces (geometry, factory, engine, detector), so it can be added to the 2D page as a model choice once it has been checked.

Probability tab

Each cell is one rock grade (row) and one value of the varied parameter (column), run with as many seeds as you set, headless in background workers (one per CPU core less one). Result per cell: share of runs with persistent blockage and with any dome, 95 % Wilson intervals, Poisson blockage rate per 1000 t discharged, time and rock discharged before blockage, where it blocks, the stones in the blocking dome, wall impacts. Replay sends any run with its seed to the Simulate tab. For pipe size against rock size, tick the grades and vary the bucket bottom ID (or bottom with the top ID scaled). Use the same seeds, duration and precision for every cell you compare.

Performance

The engine sorts stones along the axis every step and only tests pairs whose bounding spheres overlap. At High precision the time step is about 3x smaller than Standard. Expect near real time with a few stones in the window and 0.1x to 0.3x with 40 to 60 stones on a laptop; the Probability tab runs several such runs in parallel.
